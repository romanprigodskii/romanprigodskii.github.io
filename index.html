Moscow Olympiad in Economics, first. 0.7244 AUC against a bookmaker's closing line. 8,906 bouts modelled. 309 tests green at 1.0.0. Two papers under review. Seventeen.

One question runs through all of it. A model produces a number. A market produces a number. Only one of them is priced, and deciding which to believe is a statistical question with a real answer. I build the systems that produce the numbers, then the instruments that measure how much of the difference is signal.

Final year at Letovo School in Moscow, advanced mathematics track. Won the Moscow Olympiad in Economics outright on score. Four production systems shipped, two sole-authored papers under review at NeurIPS 2026 workshops.

Everything here is traceable to a committed artifact, a published competition protocol, or an official document. Where a diploma and a protocol both exist, this page cites the protocol, because a stranger can check a protocol without me.

01

Vertex MMA

A nine-model UFC forecasting stack, graded against the closing line

Live at vertexmma.com in English and Russian, source on GitHub. A 118-feature winner ensemble over LightGBM, CatBoost and logistic regression, a debut specialist, two method models, a cause-specific Poisson finish hazard, a no-intercept decision model and a ten-thousand-run simulator.

Held out from January 2025 it reaches 0.7244 AUC over 664 bouts, in a domain whose benchmark is a bookmaker who prices for a living. Against that closing line it is better calibrated and less sharp: what is left is resolution.

  • 4,581 fighters, 8,906 bouts, 795 events
  • 243k score-history rows, 86k career bouts, 31k judge scorecards
  • Point in time enforced five independent ways; the market is never a feature
  • Two dozen rejected ideas documented beside the shipped ones

02

Gluline

End-to-end encrypted messenger, four clients, shipped to 1.0.0

Live at gluline.com. A monorepo across Android in Jetpack Compose, iOS in SwiftUI, web, a Python and FastAPI backend, the infrastructure and the protocol. Ephemeral key exchange, per-message key derivation, Argon2id, elliptic-curve signatures, WebRTC peer-to-peer calls, a contextual AI assistant.

The Android client alone is roughly 251 Kotlin files and 70,000 lines. It shipped as a 1.0.0 production release only after clearing a written gate.

  • 309 unit tests passing, lintVital clean, R8 keep-rules verified
  • Two formal audits on record, April and August 2026
  • August: 74 candidate findings, 42 confirmed, 32 refuted by an adversarial second pass
  • The refutations are written down as refutations

03

Zacks to IBKR

Commissioned execution pipeline, running on a schedule

Paid client work, and the only one of these that somebody else specified. Screen ingestion from Zacks exports into an Interactive Brokers execution path: scheduled collection, VPS hosted, dry-run by default so a bad input costs nothing.

It rebalances a real account against a published rank, which means the failure mode is money and the code is written accordingly.

  • Sole developer, commissioned and paid
  • Parse, reconcile, place, reconcile again
  • Dry-run is the default, not the flag

04

Clipwell

Native clipboard history for macOS and Windows, free, MIT, on Homebrew

A small native app written twice: Swift and SwiftUI on macOS, C# and WPF on Windows. Same bar, same cards, same keys, same history format. No Electron, no account, no subscription. Installing it is brew install --cask romanprigodskii/tap/clipwell, which meant shipping a cask, a release pipeline and a product site alongside the app.

The detail I care about most is the one nobody sees: content marked concealed by a password manager is never written to disk.

  • macOS 14+, and a full Windows port
  • MIT licensed, English and Russian UI
  • Product site and source

  1. 01

    Testing by betting when the bets are real

    The null is a bookmaker's posted price, the stake is a stake, and the wealth process is the e-process. 84 pre-registered hypotheses, 1,787 priced bouts, re-audited as bets.

    E-Values: From Statistics to ML / NewInML

  2. 02

    Measure the instrument first

    An evaluation reports that a candidate improved a metric. It almost never reports the smallest improvement it could have detected, and those are different claims.

How I work

Five rules, applied across all four projects, visible in the artifacts.

  1. 01

    Measure against the hardest baseline available, and publish the comparison

    Vertex is scored against a bookmaker's closing line, the strongest benchmark the domain has, on every basis including the windows where the baseline wins.

  2. 02

    Gate before shipping

    A change ships when it clears a pre-computed detection floor and independent evaluation legs agree on its sign. Not when it looks good.

  3. 03

    Keep the rejects

    Two dozen failed ideas sit in the repository next to the shipped ones with the reason each was rejected. Official rankings twice, recency weighting, seed bagging, symmetry augmentation, a graded outcome label, post-hoc calibration.

  4. 04

    Report negative results as negative

    The e-value paper's headline finding is that the market wins, and the paper says so in its abstract rather than in a limitations section on page eight.

  5. 05

    Claim precisely

    Submitted means submitted, not published. An award cites its protocol. A number carries the window it was measured on.
