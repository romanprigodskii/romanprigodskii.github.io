Plotted here: 86 pre-registered hypotheses from the e-value audit, each at the wealth it actually earned. Six clear the bar at fair odds. One survives the bookmaker's margin.

One question runs through all of it: what can honestly be claimed about a price, and with what evidence.

I am in my final year at Letovo School in Moscow, on the advanced mathematics track, and I won the Moscow Olympiad in Economics outright on score. Outside school I have shipped four production systems and written two sole-authored papers, both under review at NeurIPS 2026 workshops.

The work keeps landing in the same place. A model produces a number. A market produces a number. Only one of them is priced, and deciding which to believe is a statistical question with a real answer. I have built the systems that produce the numbers, and then the instruments that measure how much of the difference is signal.

Everything below is traceable to a committed artifact, a published competition protocol, or an official document. Where a diploma and a protocol both exist, this page cites the protocol, because a stranger can check a protocol without me.

Live at vertexmma.com in English and Russian, source at romanprigodskii/vertex-mma. The forecaster is not one model: it is a winner ensemble over 118 features (LightGBM, CatBoost, logistic regression, blended on a validation window and order-averaged over both fighter orderings), a debut specialist, a method model, a debut method model, a cause-specific Poisson finish hazard, a no-intercept logistic decision model, and a ten-thousand-run Monte Carlo simulator.

On the held-out window from January 2025 it reaches 0.7244 AUC over 664 bouts, which is commercial grade in a domain where the benchmark is a bookmaker who prices for a living. Against that bookmaker's closing line on the 582 priced bouts, the model is better calibrated and less sharp: the remaining gap is resolution, and resolution needs information the public record does not contain. That decomposition is the result worth having, and the repository reports the comparison on every basis rather than the flattering one.

Research

Two sole-authored papers, three submissions, all under review at NeurIPS 2026 workshops. Both grew out of Vertex, and both are about the same discomfort: an evaluation that reports what it found and not what it could have found.

Paper one Under review

Testing by betting when the bets are real

An e-value audit of 84 pre-registered market-efficiency hypotheses

E-values are usually motivated by a metaphorical gambler betting against a null hypothesis. Here the gambler is not a metaphor. The null is a bookmaker's posted price, the stake is a stake, and the wealth process is the e-process. The audit covers 4,141 walk-forward out-of-fold bouts scored by models refit at 32 quarterly origins, 1,787 of which carry an archived closing price.

  1. A multiplicity correction that was not licensed

    The 84 slices are overlapping subsets of one pool. Membership correlations across the 3,486 segment pairs run from -0.49 to +0.84, half of them negative, and 83 pairs share no bouts at all. Benjamini-Hochberg needs independence or PRDS; neither is established. BH rejects four hypotheses. Benjamini-Yekutieli and e-BH, whose guarantees do hold here, reject none.

  2. "Failed to replicate" and "not yet" are different findings

    A segment the fixed-n analysis wrote off at p = 0.30 is still growing on the wealth scale: +0.0143 nats per bout against +0.0174 in the window that selected it, e = 4.81 against a threshold of 20. It is not refuted. It is 100 bouts short at fair odds and 253 at the book's, which is seventeen months, or three and a half years.

  3. Pricing the freedom a post-hoc rule used

    A threshold rule chosen after the shape was visible survives the search it actually ran, and then dies twice. The ladder is the finding, not the top rung.

Before any of it was used, the instrument was validated: under a true null with prices and stakes held and only outcomes resampled, 40,000 replications give an expected e-value of 0.985 plus or minus 0.018 per segment, with none of the 84 above its own alpha. And the headline result is negative, stated as negative in the abstract. Betting the model against the book across the whole priced pool gives e = 3.6 x 10-4 at fair odds. The paper is not about beating a market. It is about what can be honestly claimed inside a search that mostly failed.

Paper two Under review

Measure the instrument first

Detection floors for model selection and for LLM judges

An evaluation reports that a candidate improved a metric. It almost never reports the smallest improvement it could have detected, and those are different claims. This paper measures the second one with a construction that needs no distributional assumption: the null lever, the same recipe refit under a different random seed. It changes nothing real, so everything it produces is instrument noise.

  1. The noise floor is measurable, and it is not small

    On a 3,087-row walk-forward pool under five seeds, ten null comparisons manufacture effects up to 0.00207 nats. Re-seeding alone reproduces 80% of the only lever this leg ever shipped, and the pipeline had 56% power against its own shipped result.

  2. Buying seeds walks to a floor, not past it

    The floor is an asymptote at 0.00288 nats. Fifteen seeds gets within 2% of it and no budget gets past it, because 63% of the paired noise lives beyond any seed budget. On four degrees of freedom that share is bounded only to [17%, 83%], so it is reported as an estimate and not as a finding.

  3. A floor belongs to the instrument that measured the effect

    Recomputed on the 793-row pool where this project's one large win was actually measured, the floor is 0.0119 rather than 0.00362. The win is 4x its own floor, not 13x another pool's. This paper corrects that error in its own earlier draft, in print.

  4. The protocol transplants

    On a deployed LLM classifier, two identical reruns disagree on 5.6% of items, and twenty of them move measured agreement by 2.6 points. The floor is 2.9 points. Same recipe, different instrument.

How I work

Five rules, applied across all four projects, visible in the artifacts.

  1. 01

    Measure against the hardest baseline available, and publish the comparison

    Vertex is scored against a bookmaker's closing line, the strongest benchmark the domain has, on every basis including the windows where the baseline wins.

  2. 02

    Gate before shipping

    A change ships when it clears a pre-computed detection floor and independent evaluation legs agree on its sign. Not when it looks good.

  3. 03

    Keep the rejects

    Two dozen failed ideas sit in the repository next to the shipped ones with the reason each was rejected. Official rankings twice, recency weighting, seed bagging, symmetry augmentation, a graded outcome label, post-hoc calibration.

  4. 04

    Report negative results as negative

    The e-value paper's headline finding is that the market wins, and the paper says so in its abstract rather than in a limitations section on page eight.

  5. 05

    Claim precisely

    Submitted means submitted, not published. An award cites its protocol. A number carries the window it was measured on.
