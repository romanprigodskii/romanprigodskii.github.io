An e-value audit of 84 pre-registered market-efficiency hypotheses

Read the paperPDF, 287 KBShort versionPDF, 257 KB

In brief

The null hypothesis is market efficiency: the market’s closing price for a fight, with its margin taken out, is already the right probability. Each of the 84 names a segment where a good market might still be wrong, with the reason it might be. Each is tested with an e-value, a measure of evidence whose expected value stays at 1 or below while the price is right, and read twice: at fair prices, with the margin taken out, and at the prices actually posted. The audit covers 4,141 walk-forward out-of-fold bouts scored by models refit at 32 quarterly origins, 1,787 of which carry an archived closing price, first analysed with paired log-loss and Benjamini-Hochberg and then re-tested with e-values.

The standard correction did not apply

The 84 slices are overlapping subsets of one pool. Membership correlations across the 3,486 segment pairs run from −0.49 to +0.84, half of them negative, and 83 pairs share no bouts at all. Benjamini-Hochberg needs independence or PRDS; neither is established. On the 1,191-bout discovery window I actually searched, BH rejects four: one in the model’s favour and three against it. Benjamini-Yekutieli and e-BH, whose guarantees do hold here, reject none.

A segment written off too early

A segment the fixed-n analysis wrote off at p = 0.30 is still growing on the e-value scale: +0.0143 nats per bout against +0.0174 in the window that selected it, an e-value of 4.81 against a threshold of 20. It is not refuted. It is 100 bouts short at fair prices and 253 with the margin, which is seventeen months, or three and a half years.

Priced in full, a rule chosen after the fact falls short

A threshold rule chosen after the shape was visible, paid for by a mixture martingale over the 21 cuts it searched, clears the threshold at 31.28. Widen the mixture to the 21 cuts it could have searched in the opposite direction and it falls to 15.64. Add the other disagreement statistic it could have used and it falls to 10.43.

Before any of it was used, the instrument was validated. Under a true null, with the prices and the tests held fixed and only the outcomes resampled, 40,000 replications give an expected e-value of 0.985 ± 0.018 per segment, with none of the 84 above its own alpha.

The headline result is negative: tested against the market’s price across the whole priced pool, the model gives e = 3.6 × 10−4 at fair prices and 3.1 × 10−8 with the margin.

Working paper

Measure the instrument first

Detection floors for model selection and for LLM judges

Read the paperPDF, 126 KB

Abstract

An evaluation reports that a candidate improved a metric. It almost never reports the smallest improvement it could have detected, and those are different claims. This paper measures the second one with a construction that needs no distributional assumption: the null lever, the same recipe refit under a different random seed. It changes nothing real, so everything it produces is instrument noise.

The one change that shipped is hard to tell from a rerun

On a 3,087-row walk-forward pool under five seeds, ten null comparisons manufacture effects up to 0.00207 nats. Re-seeding alone reproduces 80% of the only lever this leg ever shipped, and the pipeline had 56% power against its own shipped result.

More seeds help, up to a point

More seeds lower the floor from 0.00362 nats at one seed towards an asymptote at 0.00288. Fifteen seeds get within 2% of it and no budget gets past it, because 63% of the paired noise lives beyond any seed budget. On four degrees of freedom that share is bounded only to [17%, 83%], so it is reported as an estimate and not as a finding.

Each result needs its own floor

Recomputed on the 793-row pool where this project’s one large win was actually measured, the floor is 0.0119 rather than 0.00362. The win is 4× its own floor, not 13× another pool’s.

The same test works on an LLM classifier

On a deployed LLM classifier, two identical reruns disagree on 5.6% of items, and twenty of them move measured agreement by 2.6 points. The floor is 2.9 points.

Before the papers

Collatz conjecture

Published, Young Scientist, February 2025

Halve an even number; triple an odd one and add one. The conjecture says every starting number ends at 1, and nobody has proved it. My MYP personal project was an article on it, written in English in Grade 9 with my supervisor Kirill Kusakin: it works through the residues modulo 4 and modulo 3 that a smallest counterexample would have to fall in, and reviews other authors’ approaches.

It was published in Young Scientist (Юный учёный), No. 2 (87), pp. 96–99, and Letovo named it the best MYP personal project in Mathematics.

A sausage wrapped in newspaper

School project, 2024

Cut through a sausage wrapped in newspaper, unroll the paper, and the cut edge comes out as a wave. In Grade 9 I proved why it has to be a sine: its period is the sausage’s circumference, 2πr, and its height is set by the angle of the cut. Then I checked it on a real sausage.

The 2026 revision puts a number on that check. Traced from the original scan, the edge follows a sine to within 1.8% of its height, and its height is 0.233 of its period against a predicted 0.243.

The volume of a tea glass

School project, 2025

In Grade 9 I traced a Turkish tea glass with four parabolas, cut it into discs and let the discs get infinitely thin, summing them with the formulas for sums of powers. The glass came out at 216.9 cm³. Lifted out of a measuring beaker full of water, it took between 200 and 225 ml with it.

The 2026 translation corrects one copying error, which had put the total at 208.6. Both numbers sit inside the beaker’s window; the corrected one is the one the method gives.
