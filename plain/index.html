Research

Accepted

Testing by betting when the bets are real

An e-value audit of 84 pre-registered market-efficiency hypotheses. Sole author. Accepted as a poster at two NeurIPS 2026 workshops, both non-archival: NewInML and, in a shorter version, E-Values: From Statistics to ML.

  • The null is a bookmaker’s posted price, the stake is a stake, and the wealth process is the e-process. 4,141 walk-forward out-of-fold bouts, 1,787 of them carrying an archived closing price.
  • The best of the 84 reaches e = 151. One pre-registered hypothesis needs 20; all 84 at once need 1,680.
  • The 84 slices overlap, so Benjamini-Hochberg’s guarantee is not established. Its four discoveries on the 1,191-bout discovery window, one favourable and three against the model, survive neither Benjamini-Yekutieli nor e-BH.
  • A segment written off at p = 0.30 is still growing on the wealth scale: not refuted, 100 bouts short at fair odds.
  • A threshold rule chosen after the fact clears 20 at 31.28 once the search it ran is paid for; charged for the direction it could have searched and the other disagreement statistic it could have used, it falls to 10.43, and at the prices the book offered to 2.82.
  • Validated before use: 40,000 null replications give an expected e-value of 0.985 ± 0.018 per segment. The headline result is negative.

Working paper

Measure the instrument first

Detection floors for model selection and for LLM judges. Sole author.

  • The null lever: the same recipe refit under a different random seed changes nothing real, so everything it produces is instrument noise.
  • On a 3,087-row walk-forward pool under five seeds, re-seeding alone reproduces 80% of the only change the winner model ever shipped; the pipeline had 56% power against its own shipped result.
  • Recomputed on the 793-row pool where the project’s one large win was measured, the floor is 0.0119 rather than 0.00362: the win is 4× its own floor, not 13× another pool’s.
  • The same test on a deployed LLM classifier: two identical reruns disagree on 5.6% of items, and the floor is 2.9 points of agreement.

A sausage wrapped in newspaper

A Grade 9 project, revised in 2026. Cut through a sausage wrapped in newspaper and the unrolled paper’s edge is a sine wave, with a period of 2πr and a height set by the angle of the cut. Proved, then checked on a real sausage: traced from the scan, the edge follows a sine to within 1.8% of its height (a triangle wave misses by 4.8%), and its height is 0.233 of its period against a predicted 0.243.

Vertex MMA, vertexmma.com

A nine-model UFC forecasting stack, graded against the bookmaker’s closing line. English and Russian, virtual currency only.

  • 4,581 fighters, 8,906 bouts, 795 events. 243k score-history rows, 86k career bouts, 49k ranking snapshots, 41k round-stat rows, 31k judge scorecards.
  • Nine models including a 10,000-run Monte Carlo simulator: a 118-feature winner ensemble over LightGBM, CatBoost and logistic regression, a debut specialist, method models, a cause-specific Poisson finish hazard, a no-intercept decision model.
  • Held out from January 2025: accuracy 0.6747, log-loss 0.6137, Brier 0.2124, AUC 0.7244 over 664 bouts.
  • On the 582 priced bouts, 0.6753 / 0.6171 / 0.2140 against the closing line’s 0.6838 / 0.5922 / 0.2035: better calibrated, less sharp, and the remaining gap is resolution, which needs information the public record does not contain.
  • Five separate mechanisms keep future information out of the features. Bookmaker odds are never a feature: taking them out cost 1.5 points of test accuracy, and they stayed out.
  • Roughly two dozen rejected ideas documented beside the shipped ones, each with its reason. Its evaluation is the subject of both papers above.
  • Next.js 16, React 19, TypeScript 5.9 strict, Tailwind 4, Postgres, Python 3.12. 35 routes in two languages, 1,533 translation keys each.

Concluded research

Vertex Boxing

The Vertex MMA models moved to professional boxing, to test whether regional and club lines are soft enough to beat. On every bout the available odds cover, they are not; the anonymous regional card the thesis was aimed at carries no price, so there it cannot be tested. What the model turned out to read is where the line is going.

  • Cut by level, closing-line value rises from +0.0044 on four-to-six-round club fights to +0.0180 on twelve-round title fights. On a window the rule was never chosen on, the top gave 2.0 times the closing-line value of the bottom.
  • Blended into the closing price at a weight of 0.17, the model improves its log-loss by +0.0043 nats [+0.0024, +0.0062] on 3,288 priced bouts. On its own it does not beat the closing line, and bets at the closing price make nothing.
  • At the opening price, a median three days before the fight, the blend moves the price 87% of the way to where it closes. Bet there and scored as e-values against a bar of 20 (80 for 2023–2025, registered as one of four hypotheses), the rule passed on 2016–2020 (+18% to +24% flat, its protocol timestamped in Bitcoin blocks and pushed to GitHub before the model was trained), on 2023–2025 (+10.9% at Bet365) and on the January to July 2026 holdout, at e = 41.8. On 2021–2023 it fell short, at 12.5.
  • A live test was frozen and pushed publicly on 24 September 2026, before any bout it covers had been fought: Bet365’s opening price, one unit on every side where the blend beats it, no stake placed. It can be read at any checkpoint, rejects at 20 and closes on 25 September 2029. The result will be reported either way.
  • Caught and removed a post-bell leak: judges’ scorecards are published only for fights that went the distance, so their mere presence told the model how a fight had ended. Where judges were recorded, 9.7% of fights ended early; where they were not, 74.3% did.
  • A random search over 200 configurations, under a protocol committed before the first fit: none of the five taken forward kept its lead over the model in use on bouts the search never saw.
  • All backtest, concluded September 2026. Zero real bets placed.

Alfa-Romeo

Built during an internship at Alfa-Bank, as a concept of what a bank becomes by 2035: one passport, every profile a life needs, roubles and crypto in one balance, and an AI that works as a copilot rather than a chat window. 93 screens.

  • Live rates from the Bank of Russia’s daily fixing and from CoinGecko. The wallet is a closed economy on Postgres: sign up with a phone number and send play roubles, or play crypto, to another phone.
  • The copilot is Claude behind guardrails: it drafts the action and waits for a yes, the largest action it may carry out is ₽100,000, and a deterministic stand-in takes over when there is no key or no network.
  • The credit screen shows the three factors behind the limit, then recounts it when you ask what if.
  • The business half: acquiring by payment link, QR, NFC and terminal, any payment over ₽500,000 waiting for a second signature, and an AI accountant that draws the next ninety days of cash flow and points at a gap two weeks before it opens.
